Haystack Talent

why

Executive talent is critical to scaling a company as it looks to hit its next milestone, but leadership searches are long-running and high-touch processes. Accessing top talent requires navigating close-knit networks and evaluating leaders takes domain-specific expertise. Navigating the talent marketplace to successfully hire impactful executives means coordinating across in-house recruiting, external search firms, and investor talent partners. All of these collaborators are sitting on untapped data that could accelerate and streamline executive searches.

When you're sourcing and selling executives, it comes down to who you know and if they'll respond. In an ideal world, you would be able to ask questions like "who do we know that would be a fit for this role?" and get a list of warm candidates and a script for how to reach out. This would allow everyone in the process to spend more time on the human-fit element and less on admin work.

It's a given that executive placement is a hands-on process, but more and more companies are looking to scale with frontier hires that have no precedent; as traditional role archetypes collapse and converge, more and more hiring will resemble the bespoke nature of retained search. And as public information on candidates gets commoditized and harder to pull, private data like intake notes, backchannel conversations, and reference checks becomes more critical. Tapping into this latent intel and making it actionable is how people evaluation moves from keyword search to explainable reasoning.

what

Whether you are a boutique search firm, a PE/VC talent team, or an internal exec recruiting function, you likely have an ATS for moving candidates through your workflow and a CRM serving as a candidate database. And despite those tools claiming to be systems of record, tons of work and the majority of decisions are happening through live discussion, most of which is now recorded and accessible via video conferencing and AI notetakers. The conundrum is it's difficult to move off databases that have 10+ years of work exhaust in them, and for many search firms and talent teams that annotated rolodex IS the most valuable asset.

An ideal system would live alongside the legacy ATS/CRM and could bridge the gap to modern workflow tools. Such an intelligence layer would also be accessible to provide context whenever and wherever it was needed, whether sourcing via prompt in an AI chat tool or drafting an email to a prospective candidate. The path forward is unlikely to be consolidation into one uber-tool; the best solution for talent search should acknowledge and lean into the tool fragmentation, allowing recruiters and researchers to construct a best-of-breed tech stack.

But integration is just one aspect of a recruiting co-pilot. The real secret sauce of talent placement is the matchmaking between candidate and company. A level 1 query in talent placement is "who in our network is a CxO?", but the next-level questions get into territory like "who is on the cusp of CxO that would be a great fit for company Y right now?" and "how do we know they'd be able to hit the ground running?". These types of questions require not only the integration of disparate data sources, but also an interpretation engine that can mirror the intuition of a seasoned recruiter or hiring manager. When you hire a search firm to find your next exec, what you're actually paying for is access to their institutional knowledge about the talent market. When you consult with a talent partner at your investor's firm, you're tapping into their skillset for predicting people excellence. Isn't it about time this expertise was encoded somewhere?

how

Think of the best talent magnet you know, either a hiring manager who wraps every req in record time or a recruiter who can close any candidate. Now imagine you hand this person a company dossier, a job spec, and a candidate profile. How efficiently and effectively do they find and place the right person? And what happens in between you handing them the search and the offer being signed?

These hiring mavens do a lot of pattern matching, jargon translating, and solid storytelling in a way that looks effortless but is actually a proficiency built from reading resumes, studying companies, and understanding humans. The good ones are worth their weight in gold, but it's a really hard skill to scale; the best anyone has done is to give them tools to automate the admin work and humans to offload the grunt work. But don't these excellent recruiters deserve a world-class AI co-pilot? And couldn't such an agent also make novice recruiters pretty good by making that expertise accessible?

The key insight here is that the convergence of LLMs, agentic AI, and MCP standards makes it possible to reason natively over proprietary, unstructured data at scale. Every artifact used in an exec search, from a company prospectus to a job description to a LinkedIn resume, can now be ingested and annotated with an additional layer of attributes that provides the dimensionality to do the type of matchmaking that the best recruiters are known for. An "eye for talent" can now be codified as an evaluation lens with contextual weighting, creating a model that adapts to each search uniquely instead of applying the same checklist to every role. You can now turn the tribal knowledge of your best recruiters and hiring managers into a shared skill that every employee in your org can leverage.
